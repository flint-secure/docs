Integration guide

Add device and context checks to your payment flow

Flint Secure returns a risk score and a decision for each payment, with the reasons, so your team can allow, review or decline it. This guide lists what your organisation needs to prepare, send and handle. It does not describe how Flint reaches its decisions.

You send

The payment details, the device reading from the Flint kit, and optional identity fields.

Flint returns

A score from 0 to 100, a decision (ALLOW, FLAG or BLOCK), the reasons and a transaction ID.

You decide

What your product does for each decision, and which outcomes you report back.

ScopeFlint sits beside your payment flow. It does not move money, connect to your core banking system or replace your authorisation steps.

What you need to provide

These are the items companies usually have to fix or build before Flint can score well. Check each one against your current product.

1. A place in your flow to ask for a decision

Flint needs to be called after the customer confirms a payment and before you authorise it. If you only learn about a payment after settlement, Flint can still score it for reporting, but it cannot prevent the loss.

2. A stable ID for every sender and receiver

Each payment must carry the same sender_id for the same customer every time. IDs that change per session, per app version or per channel weaken every check that depends on history. Receivers need stable IDs for the same reason.

3. The device reading from your apps

Add the Flint kit to each customer-facing app (web, Android, iOS) and pass the resulting device_id and local_fp with each payment. Payments from a channel without the kit are scored with less information.

4. Identity fields, where you are allowed to send them

The sender phone number and name, and the receiver phone number and name, improve results noticeably, in particular for number-takeover and account-sharing cases. Send numbers in international format. Phone numbers are hashed on arrival. Both phone and name must be included in your privacy notice.

5. The customer’s real IP address

If your payment API sits behind a proxy, gateway or load balancer, forward the end customer’s IP in ip_address. Otherwise Flint sees your gateway’s address and every network check is wrong.

6. Clean amounts and currency

Send amount as a number in the main currency unit and currency as a three-letter code. Mixed units across channels (for example paisa in one app and rupees in another) cause false alarms.

7. A decision handler

Your product needs a defined action for each decision: what happens on ALLOW, FLAG and BLOCK, who reviews flagged payments, and what the customer sees. See Acting on decisions.

8. A fraud outcome feed

Flint improves only when it learns what was actually fraud and what was a false alarm. Name someone on your side to send those outcomes back, daily or weekly. See Outcome feedback.

9. A fallback when Flint does not answer

Decide in advance what happens on a timeout or error. Most teams let the payment continue under their normal rules and log it. See Errors and timeouts.

10. Privacy, consent and retention terms

Your legal and compliance teams need to approve what is collected, how long it is kept and what customers are told. Flint is not used with real customer data until these terms are agreed in writing.

Where results usually go wrongChanging sender IDs, a missing customer IP behind a proxy, mixed currency units, and no outcome feedback. Fix these before you judge the accuracy of any fraud tool, ours included.

Completeness target

For the pilot, aim for identity fields (phone and name) on at least 90% of eligible payments, and for device values on every payment from an app with the kit. Your first-week report will show the actual rate, so gaps are visible early.

Integration flow

  1. The customer starts a payment in your app or checkout. The Flint kit reads the device in the background.
  2. Your app sends the payment to your server with the kit’s device_id and local_fp.
  3. Your server calls POST /v1/score with the payment details and optional identity fields.
  4. Flint answers with a score, a decision and reasons. Store the transaction_id against your own record.
  5. Your server applies your rule for ALLOW, FLAG or BLOCK, then continues or stops the payment.
  6. Later, you report outcomes (confirmed fraud or false alarm) so Flint can tune results for you.
RecommendedMake the scoring call from your server with a server-held key, not from the customer’s phone or browser. Keep API keys and signing secrets out of app code.

Authentication

Every request carries your API key in the X-API-Key header. Keys are created for you during onboarding and can be created and revoked by your administrators. Each key belongs to one organisation and can only see that organisation’s data.

POST {BASE_URL}/v1/score
X-API-Key: sk_live_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx
Content-Type: application/json

Request signing

Keys issued with a signing secret should also sign each request. Signed requests are rejected if the timestamp is more than 5 minutes off or the signature has been seen before.

body_hash  = sha256_hex(request_body)
message    = METHOD + "\n" + path + "\n" + timestamp + "\n" + body_hash
X-Timestamp: <unix seconds>
X-Signature: sha256=<hmac_sha256_hex(signing_secret, message)>

Device SDKs

The kit runs inside your own app. It needs no special permissions for its core readings. Packages and install instructions are delivered during onboarding.

PlatformMinimumWhat you call
WebModern browsersFlintFP.load({ apiKey, baseURL }) then await fp.get()
AndroidAndroid app with a ContextFlintFP.load(context, FlintOptions(...)) then get()
iOSiOS 14 and laterFlintFP.load(options:) then await get()
// Web
const fp = await FlintFP.load({ baseURL: "{BASE_URL}" });
const device = await fp.get();
// send device.deviceId and device.localFP to your server with the payment

What you need to decide

  • Where to call it. Create one kit instance per app session, not per screen, so touch and timing behaviour is captured across the whole journey.
  • Timeout. The default is 5 seconds. When the Flint service cannot be reached, the kit falls back to a local reading after the timeout. Set a shorter timeout if your checkout cannot wait.
  • Release process. Plan app-store review time for the first release that includes the kit.

Identify a device

POST /v1/identify is called by the kit to turn a device reading into a stable device ID. You normally do not call it yourself. It is documented here so your team knows what leaves the device.

Code samples

Server-side examples. Replace the placeholders with the values issued to you.

cURL (unsigned, for a first test)

curl -X POST "{BASE_URL}/v1/score" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $FLINT_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"sender_id":"user_123","amount":5000,"currency":"NPR","device_id":"dev_abc123"}'

Python (signed)

import hashlib, hmac, json, time, requests

BASE   = "{BASE_URL}"
KEY    = "sk_live_..."       # from your secret store
SECRET = "signing_secret"    # from your secret store

payload = {"sender_id": "user_123", "amount": 5000,
           "currency": "NPR", "device_id": "dev_abc123"}
body = json.dumps(payload, separators=(",", ":"))   # sign exactly what you send
path = "/v1/score"
ts   = str(int(time.time()))
msg  = f"POST\n{path}\n{ts}\n{hashlib.sha256(body.encode()).hexdigest()}"
sig  = hmac.new(SECRET.encode(), msg.encode(), hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()

r = requests.post(BASE + path, data=body, timeout=1.5, headers={
    "X-API-Key": KEY, "X-Timestamp": ts,
    "X-Signature": "sha256=" + sig,
    "Content-Type": "application/json"})
decision = r.json()["decision"] if r.ok else "FALLBACK"

Handling the answer

if decision == "BLOCK":      hold_payment(); notify_customer()
elif decision == "FLAG":     request_step_up(); queue_for_review()
else:                        continue_payment()   # ALLOW or FALLBACK, per your policy
ImportantSign the exact bytes you send. Re-serialising the JSON after signing changes the hash and the request is rejected.

Acting on decisions

This is a suggested starting design. The choices are yours.

DecisionSuggested actionWhat your team prepares
ALLOWContinue as normal.Nothing visible to the customer.
FLAGAsk for extra proof (for example a second check), delay, or send to manual review.A review queue, owners, a target response time and customer wording.
BLOCKDecline or hold the payment.A clear message, a route for the customer to appeal and a way to release holds.

Start in shadow mode, where nothing is acted on, until your team trusts the decisions.

Errors and timeouts

StatusMeaningWhat to do
200Decision returned.Apply your rule.
400Invalid request, for example missing sender_id or amount.Fix the request. Do not retry unchanged.
401Missing or invalid key, or a bad signature.Check the key, clock and signing code.
429Rate limit or monthly quota reached.Back off and retry. Contact us if it keeps happening.
5xxService problem.Use your fallback.

Flint is designed so that a problem in a supporting part of its system does not stop it from answering. Even so, your integration must be able to cope without an answer. Decide your fallback, write it down, and test it. Set a request timeout that fits your checkout, and log every payment that went through on the fallback.

Retries

  • Do not retry a 400 or 401 unchanged.
  • For a 429, wait and try again with a growing delay.
  • For a timeout or 5xx, retry at most once while the customer is waiting. If it fails again, use your fallback.
  • Keep your own payment reference on every call, so a retried payment is not counted twice in your reports.

What not to show customers

Show the reasons and breakdown to analysts only. Showing them to a customer, especially one who was blocked, teaches an attacker what to change.

Data and privacy

What Flint receives

  • Device readings: screen, hardware model, language and time zone, app version, network type and touch behaviour. Public platform interfaces only. Flint does not collect hardware serial numbers, UDIDs or MAC addresses.
  • Payment details: IDs, amount, currency and IP address.
  • Identity fields (optional): phone numbers and names. Phone numbers are hashed on arrival and raw numbers are not stored or logged.

What you are responsible for

  • A privacy notice and any customer consent required in your market.
  • Agreeing retention periods and deletion routes in writing with us.
  • Deciding which fields you are permitted to send.
  • Role-based access for your staff in the console.
Legal reviewData-protection and central-bank rules differ by country. Have your own legal and compliance teams review the terms before any real customer data is used.

Security

  • Every organisation’s data is separated from every other’s. Keys, transactions, devices, alerts and exports are visible only to the organisation that owns them.
  • Console sessions use server-side sessions in secure, HTTP-only cookies.
  • Spreadsheet exports neutralise hidden formulas.
  • Keys can be revoked immediately by your administrators.

Analyst console

Your fraud and risk staff get a web console with:

  • An overview of payments scored, allowed, flagged and blocked.
  • A searchable transaction list with filters and a detail panel showing the reasons and score breakdown.

Testing

Before going near live traffic, run these in a test environment with a test key.

  1. Happy path. A normal payment returns ALLOW.
  2. Each decision. Use the simulation scenarios below to confirm your product handles FLAG and BLOCK.
  3. Failures. Slow network, timeout, wrong one-time code and bank decline. Your fallback must behave.
  4. Bad input. Missing fields, unknown currency and a missing device ID.
  5. Signing. Wrong clock, replayed request and tampered body are rejected.

Why the blanksWe will not quote a catch rate or a before-and-after percentage for your business until it has been measured on your data. Any figure given earlier than that would be a guess. The final report shows a before-and-after for your traffic, based on your confirmed outcomes.

How the measurement works

  1. In shadow mode, Flint scores every payment in the pilot slice and nothing is blocked.
  2. Your team labels outcomes over time through outcome feedback. Fraud is often reported days or weeks later, so the report waits for a fixed cut-off date.
  3. We compare the decisions with the labels and calculate the table above.
  4. We tune thresholds for your product mix and measure again on a later period, so the result is not tuned and judged on the same data.

Questions and glossary

Does Flint replace our existing fraud rules?

No. It adds device and context information to them. Keep your own rules, and decide how they combine with Flint’s decision.

What happens if Flint is down?

Your fallback applies. Most teams continue under their normal rules and log the payment. Test this before go-live.

Will it slow our checkout?

Scoring itself took about 1 ms on the server in our tests. Your network round trip is added to that. The device reading happens in the background while the customer fills in the payment.

Can we start without sending phone numbers?

Yes. Results are better with them, but you can begin with device and payment fields and add identity fields later.

Can thresholds differ by product?

Yes. Lines for flag and block can be set for your organisation and tuned during the pilot.

Who can see our data?

Only your organisation’s users. Other customers of Flint cannot see it, and this was tested directly.

Do you have live-traffic accuracy figures?

Not yet. The pilot produces them on your traffic. Until then, the figures in this guide are test results, labelled as such.
